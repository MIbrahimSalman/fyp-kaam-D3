Chapter 5 Proposed Approach and Methodology

This chapter defines how Manzil is constructed and evaluated so that its objectives can be assessed
with controlled evidence rather than a favorable demonstration run. The method begins by freezing
definitions, exclusions, baselines, and data partitions before the final comparison. It then validates the
network, observations, controllers, routing, and corridor lifecycle in stages, because a result from an
integrated run is not interpretable when an underlying measurement or safety transition is still uncertain.
Figure 5.1 summarizes this sequence from preparation to reporting. Each stage produces an artifact or
acceptance check that is retained with the experiment manifest. The protocol also separates detector
evaluation from signal-control evaluation: recorded video supplies annotated evidence for perception,
while SUMO supplies controlled vehicle and signal state for the controller, route, and emergency exper-
iments. This separation prevents estimated video quantities from being passed off as exact network state
and allows an optional observation-noise study only after both baselines are stable.

5.2 Network and Scenario Preparation

Every controller, routing, and corridor result depends on the road network and the traffic demand placed
on it, so both are prepared and validated before any comparison. This section describes how the network
is obtained and checked, and how demand profiles and incidents are generated so that runs can be
repeated exactly.

5.2.1 Network Provenance and Validation

In FYP-II, a selected, manageable Lahore road area will be exported from OpenStreetMap under its
applicable attribution and license conditions [25]. The exact bounding box, extraction date, source file
checksum, and conversion command will be stored so that a later change in the public map cannot
silently alter an experiment. SUMO’s import toolchain will convert the graph, after which NetEdit in-
spection will verify lane counts, legal turns, junction types, traffic-light programs, pedestrian-only edges,
disconnected components, and ambulance-accessible routes. Automated checks will then attempt repre-
sentative routes, enumerate signal phases, and reject movements that conflict with the intended vehicle
classes. The initial planning range is approximately 10–20 signalized intersections, but this is not a
confirmed network fact or a requirement to retain a defective import. A smaller validated extract is
preferable to a larger inaccurate one because route continuity and lawful signal control are prerequisites
for every later result. If the chosen map cannot be stabilized within the schedule, a documented syn-
thetic multi-intersection grid becomes the fallback, and all results are identified as synthetic rather than
generalized to Lahore roads.

The prototype uses this synthetic grid as a controlled test track, and the Lahore extract follows in FYP-II.
It is a 3×3 grid generated with SUMO’s network generator, with 200 m blocks, two lanes per direction,
50 km/h, and nine signalized junctions. All code reads junctions, phases, lanes, and connections from
the network file, so moving to Lahore requires map cleanup rather than code changes.

5.2.2 Demand and Incidents

Demand files define light, medium, and heavy conditions together with an uneven-approach case that
can expose starvation. Vehicle insertion uses explicit random seeds, and a declared warm-up interval
allows traffic to populate the network before measurement begins. Incident scenarios close an edge,
reduce lane capacity, or introduce a controlled obstruction at a fixed simulation time. A failed insertion
caused by a full lane is retained as an outcome rather than retried until the run appears favorable. The
same requested demand and seed are replayed across controllers where SUMO semantics permit. When
controller behavior changes route choice or insertion feasibility, the manifest distinguishes the original
demand request from actual departures so that throughput is not interpreted without its denominator.
Scenario families are separated into development, tuning, and held-out evaluation sets before final runs.
Origin-destination pairs, incident timing, and emergency dispatch time are also matched within each
comparison. These controls make differences attributable to the selected policy while preserving con-
gestion effects that legitimately arise from that policy. In the implementation, demand is generated by
a seeded Poisson process per entry road at 200, 400, and 600 vehicles per hour for light, medium, and
heavy demand, and 800 north–south against 100 east–west for the uneven profile.

result. The detector produces a class, confidence, and image-space box; the tracker associates obser-

queue proxy, and a speed proxy with source time and confidence. Held-out annotations feed the error-
analysis path and are never used to tune the final evaluation sequence. A speed in physical units requires
camera calibration or a defensible known scale. Without that evidence, the system reports pixel dis-
placement or a categorical slow or stationary state rather than kilometres per hour. The figure therefore
emphasizes where uncertainty enters the pipeline and why an apparently stable box is not yet a validated
traffic measure.

The detector is initialized from a documented pretrained checkpoint, consistent with the practical mo-
tivation of YOLO-family detection [7]. ByteTrack is used as the tracker because its association of
lower-confidence boxes can reduce fragmentation under temporary occlusion [2]. DeepSORT remains
an alternative if appearance information is needed in local scenes [8]. Detector and tracker settings are
fixed before held-out evaluation; FYP-I uses ByteTrack’s default thresholds. Selection considers inter-
val count error, track fragmentation when identity is required, queue-proxy error, processing throughput,
and failure by scene condition; benchmark reputation alone is insufficient. Equation (5.1) defines image
occupancy when calibration is unavailable, using the fraction of a declared region covered by tracked
boxes across sampled frames. Because perspective and occlusion affect that fraction, it is reported as
a proxy with its region geometry and sampling interval, not renamed as physical road density. This
evaluation keeps the claimed output aligned with what the video can actually support.

The implemented pipeline fine-tunes YOLO11s on UA-DETRAC frames together with COCO images
of motorcycles, tracks with ByteTrack, and counts a vehicle once when its box centre crosses a line at
60 percent of the image height. True counts are produced by passing the annotated tracks through the
same counter, so the count error measures detection and tracking only. Occupancy and speed proxies
are not computed in FYP-I.
