Chapter 10 Conclusion and Future Work

This chapter concludes the FYP-I phase of Manzil. It summarizes what the project has shown, records
the work completed against the objectives of Chapter 2, and sets out the plan for FYP-II.

10.1 Conclusion

Manzil set out to study, in a controlled and repeatable setting, how observable traffic conditions can
support signal control, how an ambulance route can respond to current delay, and how an ambulance can
receive temporary signal priority without unreasonable harm to ordinary traffic. During FYP-I the team
built a working prototype around SUMO in which every signal change passes through one safety filter,
and measured each component against a matched baseline on held-out seeds.

The results are encouraging within their limits. On the synthetic grid, the parameter-sharing DQN
reduced mean travel time by 9 to 11 percent and waiting time by 24 to 40 percent against tuned fixed-
time control, at the cost of longer red times for minor phases. Live travel-time routing roughly halved
the ambulance’s travel time around an obstruction, and the moving corridor reduced it by 34 to 59
percent while ordinary-traffic queues rose by about 3 percent. The fine-tuned detector reached 0.799
mAP50 on UA-DETRAC test data, and the counting chain had a mean error of 2.60 vehicles per clip.
No experiment produced an unsafe signal state, and the ambulance never crossed a red light. The work
also showed the value of measuring before claiming: a misleading travel-time metric, a starved phase
under the DQN, and a corridor defect that made the ambulance meet red lights were each found through
recorded evidence and fixed. The evidence is from a synthetic grid and public video, so it shows that the
approach works on a controlled test track; showing that it holds on Lahore roads is the task of FYP-II.

In summary, the following work was completed:
    • a reproducible 3×3 SUMO grid with four seeded demand profiles and a network reader that works
       on any SUMO network;
    • a single simulation adapter, a safety filter with a maximum-red guard, and an independent safety
       monitor;
    • fixed-time, pressure-based adaptive, and parameter-sharing Double DQN controllers, with the
       DQN trained in 17 minutes on CPU;
    • time-based ambulance routing with Dijkstra and an admissible A*, and closure- and improvement-
       triggered rerouting;
    • a moving green corridor with a 5 s corridor minimum green, and a per-second record of why the
       ambulance stands still;
    • a vehicle detection, tracking, counting, and queue-estimation pipeline trained and evaluated on
       Kaggle GPUs;
    • a FastAPI service and a React dashboard for live runs with ambulance dispatch and road closure;
    • batch and comparison tools with paired statistics, and 147 automated tests, all passing.
