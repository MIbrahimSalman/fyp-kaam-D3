3.4 Conclusion

The review supports a staged technical strategy. Fixed-time and pressure-inspired controllers must op-
erate correctly before comparison with a learned controller, and measurement definitions must be fixed
before an optimization result is interpreted. Video experiments should assess the complete observation
chain rather than quote detector accuracy as a traffic-management result. Simulation experiments should
use matched seeds, identical safety constraints, and held-out scenarios so controller differences are not
confused with different demand. Emergency evaluation must separate route selection from signal prior-
ity because combining both changes would obscure the source of any improvement. The cited literature
justifies the selected building blocks but does not establish their performance for Manzil’s network,
cameras, or computing resources. It also requires results to remain bounded to the tested viewpoints,
traffic demand, simulator settings, and route conditions. Chapter 4 converts these findings into traceable
requirements, while Chapter 5 defines the measurements and controlled comparisons needed to address
the evaluation objectives without claiming field deployment or certified safety evidence. The FYP-I im-
plementation followed this strategy, and Chapter 9 reports where its results agree or disagree with the
expectations drawn from the literature.
