9.7 Discussion

The results support the main idea of the project within the tested conditions. A learned signal controller
can reduce travel and waiting time compared with a well-tuned fixed plan, and live travel-time routing
and a bounded corridor each give large reductions in ambulance delay, with small measured cost to ordi-
nary traffic and no unsafe signal state in any run. The two emergency mechanisms are complementary:
routing avoids a slow road, while the corridor removes waiting at signals on the chosen road. The per-
ception chain counts vehicles with an overall count error (WAPE) of about 12 percent on public video,
which is good enough to indicate demand but not yet good enough to replace simulator state in control
experiments.

The results also show trade-offs that a single demonstration would hide. The DQN’s gain comes partly
from making minor phases wait longer, and only the safety filter’s maximum-red guard keeps that wait
bounded; a fairness term in the reward or a lower guard would cost some of the travel-time gain. The
adaptive controller lowers waiting and queues but not total travel time against a tuned plan, which shows
that a responsive rule is not automatically better than a good fixed plan. In perception, fine-tuning helped
cars and buses but not trucks, which are rare in the training data, and could not improve motorcycles
without local labels.

The main limits of the evidence are as follows. All simulation results come from one synthetic grid with
one ambulance origin and destination, so they show behaviour on a controlled test track, not on Lahore
roads. Five seeds were used instead of ten, and training and evaluation use the same four demand profile
types on different seeds; there is no held-out demand pattern or second network yet. The DQN was
trained once, so the variation between training runs is unknown. The corridor’s downstream-capacity
check and the compensation plan during recovery are not implemented, and the 5 s corridor result is
a development-stage estimate as explained above. The detector has not been tested on Lahore video,
which contains vehicle types and camera angles absent from UA-DETRAC. These limits define the
FYP-II work in Chapter 10.

9.8 Conclusion

The FYP-I experiments produced measured, repeatable evidence for every main component of Manzil.
The fine-tuned detector reached 0.799 mAP50 on UA-DETRAC test data, and the counting chain had

a mean error of 2.60 vehicles per clip. On the synthetic grid the DQN reduced travel time by 9 to 11
percent against tuned fixed-time control at a cost in fairness to minor phases, dynamic routing roughly

59 percent with little cost to ordinary traffic. Each result is bounded by the network, data, and seeds on
which it was measured, and the next chapter turns those bounds into the FYP-II plan.
